\label{sec:XML:AppExactPullback}

Proposition~\ref{prop:PullbackExact} says that for a linear model over additive features the pullback of the model's
Shapley values is, exactly, the Shapley values of the whole pipeline over the bytes. The experiments of the main text
use a gradient-boosted tree, which is nonlinear, so none of them runs under the proposition's conditions. We therefore
check the proposition directly, in the smallest setting that satisfies it.

We train a logistic regression on the additive feature groups of EMBER (fields, counts, histograms, and hashed counts,
the groups whose instrumented provenance is the Shapley provenance; Table~\ref{tab:XML:Conventions}), leaving out the
entropies and averages that follow the data-flow convention. For such a model the pipeline's Shapley value for a byte
has a closed form, since the score is a weighted sum of per-feature games that are each additive over the feature's
items: byte $z_i$ receives $\sum_j \beta_j$ times its recorded contribution to feature $x_j$. We compute this directly
from the recorded contributions, and separately pull the model's feature-space Shapley values ($a_j = \beta_j \Delta_j$,
against the baseline of no items present) back through $P$. The two coincide to numerical precision
\synthetic{(maximum difference $10^{-12}$ of a log-odd over the whole explanation set)}, as the proposition states. The
check needs no sampling and runs on every file; it is a consistency check between two code paths that share the same
recorded contributions, not an independent re-derivation, and it catches a wrong normalization of $P$, a baseline used
inconsistently between the explainer and the pullback, or a mistake in how items map to bytes.

The check also lets us measure what we give up on the other features. The entropies and averages are not additive, so
for them $P$ records the data flow rather than the Shapley provenance (Section~\ref{sec:XML:Constructing}), and the
proposition does not promise an exact pullback there. To see how far off it is, we build, for these features only, the
provenance the proposition would need, by sampling each feature's Shapley shares over its items, and pull the same
linear model back through it. That gives two byte-level attributions for the non-additive features: the one from our
data-flow $P$, which we use everywhere, and the exact one. They mostly land on the same bytes, agreeing on
\synthetic{a median of $0.74$} of the attribution and differing on the remaining quarter. And because the non-additive
features carry only a small share of the attribution to begin with (Section~\ref{sec:XML:SetupEMBER}), that quarter is
a small part of the whole file's. In exchange, the convention avoids a Shapley computation for every such feature on
every file. We keep it for the reasons in Section~\ref{sec:XML:Constructing}; here we only put a number on its cost.
